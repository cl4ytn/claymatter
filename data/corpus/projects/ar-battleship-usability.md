## AR Battleship Usability Testing Setup

Before each usability testing session for AR Battleship, I structured the opening statements to set expectations and record feedback. I started with a recording notice to get participant consent for screen and audio recording. Next, I provided a think-aloud briefing, asking users to voice whatever came to mind, what they looked at, what they tried to do, and what felt confusing or surprising with no wrong answers. Finally, I established context framing, explaining that the game was a version of Battleship and that the buttons represented actions destined for an augmented reality experience.

## AR Battleship Task 1: Single-Player Entry Point

For Task 1, I instructed participants to go ahead and start a single-player game however it felt natural. As a facilitator, I noted whether participants found the single-player entry point without hesitation, with scanning, or with backtracking. Upon completion, I checked in by asking if the experience matched their expectations and if anything was surprising.

## AR Battleship Task 2: Ship Placement Strategy

For Task 2, I prompted participants to place all their ships. Before they acted, I asked for their first instinct on how to go about placement and what information on the screen helped them decide where to start. During the task, I checked if it worked as expected. After completion, I asked if placement worked how they imagined and if there was anything they wanted to do that they couldn't. As a facilitator, I recorded placement strategies as either sequential or strategic, noted any rotation confusion, and checked whether grid feedback like valid or invalid placement indicators was understood.

## AR Battleship Task 3: Taking the First Shot

For Task 3, I had participants take their turn. Before they acted, I asked what they thought they were able to do and what the first thing they would try was. After their first shot, I asked what the result told them and whether it matched their expectations. As a facilitator, I observed whether hit or miss feedback was immediately understood, whether participants scanned previous shots to inform strategy, and noted any comments regarding spatial orientation.

## AR Battleship Task 4: Gameplay and Turn State

For Task 4, I asked participants to play through until the game ended while continuing to think aloud. During the task, I asked for their current read on the game, whether there was anything they wished they could see but couldn't, and if anything felt like it would make more sense in physical space. As a facilitator, I observed whether participants lost track of turn state, missed opponent damage feedback, or expressed confusion about win and loss conditions.

## AR Battleship Task 5: Tag Tracking and Input

For Task 5, I instructed participants to take their turn using tags. Before they acted, I asked how they would use the given tags to play the game and what they would try first. After the first shot, I asked if the outcome matched what they expected to happen. As a facilitator, I observed whether participants experienced difficulty using tags to track input.

## AR Battleship End-Game Interview

During the end-game interview, I walked through experience, comprehension, AR integration, and satisfaction checks. For the experience check, I asked users to walk through their overall experience, what stood out, and moments where things clicked or didn't. For comprehension, I asked if they always knew whose turn it was, if button functions were clear before pressing, and if they had to figure out rules on their own. For the AR check, I asked if the AR feature felt natural or like a stretch, what multiplayer features the AR missed, and what belonged in a physical space rather than on a screen. I concluded by asking about satisfaction, whether they would play again, what would make it more compelling, and opened the floor for any final observations.

## AR Battlesion System Usability Scale

As a final step in the usability testing session, I showed participants a QR code linking to a short survey containing 10 quick statements about the experience, asking them to rate each statement honestly.
